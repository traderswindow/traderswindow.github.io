What is the funding rate in crypto?

The funding rate is the small payment that keeps perpetual futures close to the spot price. It also tells you which side of the market is crowded.

Why perpetual futures need funding

A regular futures contract has an expiry date, and on that date its price has to meet the spot price. Perpetual futures never expire, so exchanges use another mechanism to keep them anchored: every few hours, traders on one side pay traders on the other.

On Binance most perpetual contracts settle funding every 8 hours, at 00:00, 08:00 and 16:00 UTC. Some contracts settle more often. You only pay or receive funding if you hold a position at the moment it settles.

Who pays whom

FundingWhat happensWhat it usually means
PositiveLongs pay shortsPerpetuals trade above spot; more traders want to be long.
NegativeShorts pay longsPerpetuals trade below spot; more traders want to be short.

The payment is a percentage of your position size. At a funding rate of 0.01%, a $10,000 long pays $1 at each settlement, about $3 a day. At 0.1% it pays $10 each time, $30 a day, which adds up quickly with leverage.

How to read it

  • Around +0.01% is the neutral baseline on most Binance contracts. Nothing unusual.
  • Well above that (0.05% and higher) means longs are paying a lot to stay in. Positioning is crowded, and a drop can trigger a wave of long liquidations.
  • Negative funding in a rising market often means shorts are fighting the trend, which is the setup for a short squeeze.
  • A flip from positive to negative, or back, is a change in sentiment on futures worth noticing.

Funding describes positioning, not timing. Crowded trades can stay crowded for days. Treat extreme funding as a reason for caution, not as a signal to take the other side.

Funding in Traders Window

Under each coin, F shows the current funding rate and a countdown to the next settlement, for example F +0.0052% 5h18. It turns amber above 0.05%. You can turn on an alert for when funding flips sign, with its own sound.
