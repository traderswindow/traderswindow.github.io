Crypto trading sessions: when Asia, London and New York are open

Crypto never closes, but it isn't equally busy all day. Volume and big moves tend to cluster around the hours when the traditional markets open.

Why sessions matter in a 24/7 market

Many of the people and firms who trade crypto also trade stocks, bonds and currencies, and they keep office hours. Economic data, like US inflation and jobs reports, comes out at fixed times. So even though Bitcoin trades every minute of the year, liquidity and volatility have a daily rhythm.

Session hours

These are the regular hours of the main stock markets in each region. The UTC times shift when Europe and the US change their clocks; Japan doesn't use daylight saving time.

Europe and the US don't change their clocks on the same weekend, so for a couple of weeks in March and in late October or early November the gap between London and New York is one hour shorter than usual.

The busiest windows

  • The London open often brings the first real move of the day after the quieter Asian hours.
  • The London–New York overlap, about two hours each weekday, is typically the most liquid part of the day.
  • US data releases, usually at 08:30 New York time, and the US stock open at 09:30 can move crypto sharply within minutes.
  • Weekends are thinner. With fewer participants, a single large order can move price further than it would on a weekday.

These are tendencies, not rules. News, liquidations and large players can move the market at any hour.

Other daily times worth knowing

  • 00:00 UTC is the daily open on most exchanges and charts. Many traders use it as a reference level; see how the widget shows it as D.
  • 00:00, 08:00 and 16:00 UTC are the usual funding settlements on Binance perpetuals.
  • Monday 00:00 UTC is the weekly open, shown as W.
